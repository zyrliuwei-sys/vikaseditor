<h2 id="which-logo">{'Start with the exact website or creator'}</h2>

A familiar name or a large letter V is not enough to identify an asset. Check the address of the page you are describing. If your article is about this site, the charcoal square with the warm gold V and corner brackets is the appropriate mark. If you mean a different creator, get that creator's artwork and usage instructions from their own page.

The [Vikasheditor guide](/guides/vikasheditor) explains the distinction between similarly named resources. For a tutorial about photo creation here, the [Vikas Editor starting guide](/guides/vikas-editor) gives you the matching product workflow to link beside the mark.

Avoid extracting a logo from a small search thumbnail. It may be compressed, cropped, outdated or attached to the wrong result. A source file from the site being described is easier to verify and produces a cleaner layout.

If an editor will not accept SVG, use one of the PNG downloads above. The 512px versions suit small screen placements; choose the 1024px card when you need a larger raster image. For a different size, export from the SVG with a square canvas and the aspect ratio locked. Enlarging a small PNG cannot restore the original edge detail.

<h2 id="place-the-logo">{'Give the mark room to remain readable'}</h2>

Start with the original proportions. Do not squeeze the square into a wide rectangle or crop out the corner brackets. As a practical layout recommendation, leave empty space around it equal to roughly **one quarter of the mark's width**. The diagram below illustrates that spacing; it is a layout aid, not a downloadable replacement logo.

For a tutorial thumbnail, place the logo away from the person's face and the main headline. On a light page, the built-in charcoal square supplies its own contrasting background. On a dark page, leave enough separation that its shape is still clear. Review the final composition at the size people will actually see, especially on a phone.

For accessibility, an image-only link to the site needs a name such as “Vikas Editor AI home.” If the same link already contains that visible text, its adjacent logo can have empty alternative text to avoid repeating the name. Describe the asset when the artwork itself is the subject of an article, as it is here.

<h2 id="logo-or-watermark">{'A logo is not a watermark-removal tool'}</h2>

A site logo identifies a service. A watermark is an element placed on an image or video. A creator signature identifies the person who made or shared a work. Downloading one site's logo does not remove any of those elements from an existing picture.

If your goal is to make a clean composition, begin with a photo you may use and ask for a scene without added lettering. Review the output because a prompt does not guarantee perfect text handling. The [prompt guide](/guides/vikas-editor-prompt) shows how to describe a scene clearly; it does not provide a way to remove someone else's attribution.
